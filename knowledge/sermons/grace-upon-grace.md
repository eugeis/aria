# Grace Upon Grace

**Script — sermon for 2026-05-10 (John 1:14–17)**

> And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory…
> And of his fullness have all received, grace upon grace.
> — John 1:14, 16

## Theme

Grace is not a feeling. It is a fullness — the kind of fullness that a river
bed has when the dam gives way. "Grace upon grace" (charin anti charitos) is
the genitive of fullness: grace coming from grace, wave after wave, without
explanation and without limit.

## Point 1 — The law brings a syllable, the grace a word

Moses gave the law by hand; what came through Jesus Christ is grace and truth.
The law is a syllable — necessary, sharp, pointing. The word is the whole
sentence: context, intention, end. Law without grace is a syllable repeated
until it becomes noise. Grace without law is a word without grammar — sweet
and unformed. The fullness holds both.

## Point 2 — Receiving, not earning

"Have all received" — the aorist of the verb: they took what was offered.
Nothing in the verse says they qualified. The Greek is the same word the
tax collector used when he collected the toll: he received the coin that was
laid in his hand. The order of the hands is the order of grace: the open hand
first, the coin second.

## Point 3 — Upon: the pile, not the patch

The preposition says "upon" — not a patch over the torn garment, but a layer
on a layer. Each grace does not cancel the last; it stands on it. The man who
was forgiven and forgave again: the second forgiveness is the first grace
receiving its own shape. That is why grace accumulates where the law cannot:
the law is a line you fall from; grace is a floor you build on.

## Application

Somewhere in this week you have already received one grace you have not
thanked. Find it tonight. Name it out loud. The naming is not the payment —
the payment is still not yours. The naming is where the "upon" begins.

*Prayer: Lord, we have seen the glory — the quiet kind, the kind that stays.
Keep giving us the fullness. Amen.*
